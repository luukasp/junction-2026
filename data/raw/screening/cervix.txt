Screening programme overview
– The national cervical cancer screening programme has been running
since the 1970s. In 2024 the screening method was either HPV-testing
(92%) or Pap testing (8%) depending on the wellbeing services
county.
– The target population, i.e., ages 30 to 65, were invited for
routine screening at ages divisible by five. In some municipalities
those aged 25 were also invited. In addition, those who had a mild
positive screening result (HPV-positivity, ASC-US, LSIL) in the previous
screening round and were not referred to further examinations, received
an invitation for risk group screening.
– 280,812 persons were drawn from the Digital and Population Data
Services Agency database for the screening invitation. An invitation may
not be sent, for example due to a missing address resulting from a
non-disclosure restriction.
– Out of the target population in 2024, 73% attended screening. In
routine screening 5.6% were recommended risk group screening and 1.2%
were referred for further examinations. In risk group screening 1.3%
received a recommendation to another risk group screen and 44.2% were
referred to further examinations.
– Based on the colposcopies performed as further examinations,
altogether 43 cervical cancers and 1,448 severe precancers were
detected.

Details
Data
The data includes all persons eligible for screening, and the
screening-chain related findings reported on them by spring 2026.
Only screening registry data has been used in the classification of
findings, i.e. the figures do not include cancer findings outside the
screening programme that are later linked from the cancer registry.
Cancer registry data has also been utilised in the
detection-mode-specific classifications.
Indicator definitions
The participation rate was calculated by dividing the successful
tests (at most one per person) with the number of persons who were
invited. Since 2020, due to limitations in registration, the number of
persons invited might be slightly smaller/larger than reported
here.
Screening results are classified as the most severe result of the
person within a screening episode.
The confidence intervals in positivity rates and attendance rates
model the uncertainty related to size of the population and random
variation. The intervals are calculated using exact binomial model and
95% confidence.
Population classification
The statistics production uses Statistics Finland’s individual-level
data on education under permit TK/2743/07.03.00/2023.
Education categories were created by combining the categories of
pre-primary, lower primary, and upper primary education into the primary
education category, combining secondary education and special vocational
qualifications into the secondary education category, and combining
lower and upper tertiary education and doctoral education into the
tertiary education category. Unknown and missing data were combined with
the primary education category. The education data is from the year
preceding the screening year (e.g. for screening year 2024, the
education data is from 2023).
Native language and area of birth are based on data from the Digital
and Population Data Services Agency.
Result classification
Normal: The next invitation will be sent after 5 years until age
65.
Borderline: The result requires a follow-up screen (new sample)
after 18-24 months (HPV+, ASC-US, LSIL with no referral).
Referral: The result requires a referral to colposcopy or a referral
has been given.
HPV tested: The number and proportion of women screened by the HPV
test of the screened women. Overlaps with the other result
categories.
HPV positive: The number and proportion of HPV positives of women
who were screened by the HPV test. Overlaps with the other result
categories.
Cervical cancer: Invasive and microinvasive carsinomas of the
cervix.
AIS: Adenocarcinoma in situ.
HSIL: Histological HSIL, high-grade squamous intraepithelial
lesion.
LSIL: Histological LSIL, low-grade squamous intraepithelial
lesion.
Normal/benign: Normal or benign findings.
No further examinations: The person did not attend colposcopy or
data has not been delivered to the cancer registry.
Other/unknown: Result is missing/inadequate; No biopsy was taken;
Other cancers or precancers (primary site other than cervix).
Histological HSIL+: HSIL, AIS, or cervical cancer.
Colposcopy done: The person attended colposcopy and data was
delivered to the cancer registry. This information has been available
since around 2022.
Cancer detection mode
The cancer detection mode categories are defined as follows, taking
into account both age- and risk-group screening:
Screened cancer: In the preceding screen within one year before the
diagnosis, a referral to further examinations was made and a biopsy was
taken.
Interval cancer:
In the preceding screen within 5.5 years before the diagnosis, no
referral to further examinations was made and no biopsy was taken.
In the preceding screen within 5.5 years before the diagnosis, a
referral to further examinations was made and a biopsy was taken, but
the date of diagnosis is 1–5.5 years after the screening test.
Unattended: Invited to screening within 5.5 years before the
diagnosis, but did not attend.
Not invited: In screening age, but no registered invitation within
5.5 years before the diagnosis.
Younger than screening age: Diagnosis received before screening
age.
Older than screening age: Diagnosis received more than 5.5 years
after passing screening age.
