Screening programme overview
– In 2024, the target population for colorectal cancer screening —
individuals turning 60, 62, 64, 66, 68 or 70 during the year — were
invited. Together with the invitation letter, a self-sampling kit for
faecal immunochemical testing (FIT) was sent to all eligible
individuals.
– 421,365 individuals were selected from the Digital and Population
Data Services Agency database for a screening invitation, of whom
419,966 (99.7%) were sent an invitation by post. An invitation may not
be sent, for example due to a non-disclosure order or missing address.
Åland is not part of the national programme and is not included in these
statistics.
– Approximately 73.5% of the 2024 target population returned the
sampling kit. Of these, 4.5% had a blood content in their stool
exceeding the positive test threshold (25 micrograms of blood per gram
of stool).
– Those with positive test results were referred to a screening nurse
for further evaluation. The primary follow-up examination was
colonoscopy, which was performed on 10,244 screening participants.
Alternative CT colonographies were reported for 128 persons.
– In the 2024 screening, 413 cancers and 3,824 follow-up-requiring or
advanced colorectal cancer precursors were detected.
– In 2024, individuals were invited to a second screening round for
the first time nationwide. This was reflected in a decrease in the
proportion of positives, a decrease in the proportion of detected
cancers and precursor lesions, and a reduced number of colonoscopies
compared with the previous year.
– The national screening programme began in 2022. This was preceded
by a pilot in 12 municipalities from 2019 to 2021.

Interval cancers
In the interval cancer statistics, cancers reported to the Cancer Registry during the screening round have been linked to each person eligible for screening (year 2022). Screen-detected cancers are cases where a test-positive individual has undergone colonoscopy and a precancerous lesion or cancer was found during colonoscopy. Interval cancers among positives are cancers in individuals who have not undergone a screening colonoscopy. A more detailed definition can be found in the section Definitions of Indicators at the bottom of the page.

Episode sensitivity
Details
Data
The data includes all of the persons eligible for screening in
2022–24, and their screening-chain diagnoses reported by 15 May
2026.
Indicator definitions
The participation rate was calculated by dividing the number of
persons who successfully returned the test (at least one successful test
result per round) by the number of persons selected for the screening
invitation. The selected persons also include those who were eligible to
participate in screening but were not sent an invitation, for example
because of a missing address due to a non-disclosure order.
Test positivity rate was calculated by dividing the number of
positives by the number of people who returned at least one test
successfully.
The median delay to colonoscopy describes the delay within which
half of the test-positive persons are admitted to colonoscopy. The upper
limit of the first decile is the limit within which 10% of test
positives are admitted to colonoscopy. Correspondingly, the lower limit
of the 10th decile describes the delay that is exceeded by 10% of test
positives.
The confidence intervals for the participation rate and the
proportion of positives describe the uncertainty in the proportion
arising from sample size and random variation. The confidence intervals
are calculated as exact 95%–confidence intervals based on a binomial
model.
In the interval cancer statistics, the cancers detected during the
screening round have been linked to each person eligible for screening
in 2022 by combining cancer registry and mass screening registry data.
Screen-detected cancers comprise cases where a test-positive person
underwent colonoscopy, an adenoma or cancer was detected at the
colonoscopy, and the person has a cancer diagnosis either in the mass
screening registry or in the cancer registry before the start of the
next screening round. Interval cancers among test positives are cancers
detected in test-positive persons who did not attend colonoscopy within
the screening programme. Interval cancers among negatives and
non-participants are, for these persons, cancers detected on the basis
of the cancer registry before the start of the next screening round. The
statistics use both mass screening and cancer registry data, which is
why the figures differ from the cancer figures in diagnosis data for
those who underwent colonoscopy in the 2022 screening round.
Episode sensitivity is calculated by dividing the screen-detected
cancers by the sum of the screen-detected cancers and the interval
cancers among negatives.
Previous-round results use additional data on the screening history
of persons who resided in the pilot areas of the 2019–21 screening
pilot.
Population classification
Statistics production uses individual-level education data released
by Statistics Finland under permit TK/2743/07.03.00/2023.
The education categories were created by combining the categories
pre-primary, lower primary and upper primary into the category primary
education, by combining secondary education and specialist vocational
qualifications into the category secondary education, and by combining
lower and upper tertiary education and doctoral education into the
category tertiary education. Unknown and missing data have been combined
with primary education in the same category. The education data refer to
the year preceding the screening year (e.g. for the 2024 screening year,
the education data are from 2023).
Native language and area of birth are based on data from the Digital
and Population Data Services Agency.
Diagnosis classification
Diagnoses are classified at the screening-round level, according to
the person’s most severe diagnosis during the round.
No CRC, adenomas or polyps covers all screening rounds for which no
codes beginning with M8 in the ICD-O-3 classification, nor SNOMED codes
for polyps (M7200/0, M7204/0, M7564/0 or M7680/0), have been reported,
and for which there is no notation of a polyp or tumour in the
colonoscopy quality data.
Adenomas and polyps include all adenomas, sessile serrated lesions
and hyperplastic polyps, covering ICD-O-3 codes beginning with M8 up to
those beginning with M85, and SNOMED codes M7200/0, M7204/0, M7564/0 or
M7680/0.
Advanced adenomas/findings requiring follow-up include all adenomas
with high-grade dysplasia, all polyps with a diameter greater than 10
mm, or cases where four or more polyps were found.
Cancers include all tumours classified as malignant according to the
ICD-O-3 classification, including rarer tumours (e.g. sarcomas).
Others/poorly defined include those cases where a polyp or tumour
from the colonoscopy stage has been reported to the mass screening
registry, but no more detailed classification or, for example, size is
known for the polyp on the basis of which it would be known to require
follow-up. The category also covers rarer non-malignant tumours (ICD-O-3
M8600–M9992, e.g. lipomas).
Statistics are supplemented retrospectively after review, including
with regard to diagnosis codes. Screening data are updated
retrospectively, which is why detection proportions, for example, may
change in later statistical publications.
For the classification of diagnoses among persons who underwent
colonoscopy, only mass screening registry data have been used. Diagnoses
outside screening have been analysed by combining cancer registry and
mass screening registry data in the Special statistics subsections
Interval cancers and Episode sensitivity.
